## Answer Key

| # | Answer | Explanation |
|---|--------|-------------|
| 1 | B | CEFR defines C2 by ability to differentiate finer shades of meaning, not by zero errors or vocabulary size. |
| 2 | C | "I'm responsible for this project" is natural and professional. Option A is grammatically correct but stilted. |
| 3 | C | "Science" (zero article) for generic reference to the concept. "The science" would need specification. |
| 4 | B | "The English" because it's specified ("spoken in Singapore"). Generic "English" wouldn't have "the". |
| 5 | B | Appropriate hedging ("might be", "could benefit") without being excessively indirect (C) or too direct (A, D). |
| 6 | C | "One way of looking at it" typically implies disagreement or skepticism phrased politely. |
| 7 | B | Academic register requires formal vocabulary and nominalization ("efficacy", "empirically validated"). |
| 8 | B | Negative inversion is marked for emphasis. It's grammatical but draws attention—use when emphasis is intended. |
| 9 | B | Fossilized errors are mistakes that have become automatic through repetition and resist correction. |
| 10 | A | Russian speakers often add "the" before abstracts ("the life is hard") due to L1 transfer. |
| 11 | C | "I'll bear that in mind" is often a polite dismissal, not a commitment to action. |
| 12 | B | "Crucial" is already an extreme adjective (like "unique", "essential"). "Very" is redundant with extreme adjectives. |
| 13 | B | Formal register is appropriate for business proposals. Frozen is for legal/ceremonial texts. |
| 14 | B | Descriptively, "who" in object position is standard spoken English, though prescriptive grammar prefers "whom". |
| 15 | C | C2 writing varies structure and uses appropriate hedging. Simplicity alone is not C2; nor is complexity without purpose. |
| 16 | A | "Deeply disappointed" is a natural collocation. "Very disappointed" is acceptable but less sophisticated. |
| 17 | B | Indirectness in requests respects the listener's autonomy (their right to refuse). It's a politeness strategy. |
| 18 | B | The receptive-productive gap means you understand structures in input that you don't naturally produce yourself. |
| 19 | B | Notice → Discriminate → Analyse → Produce. Noticing comes first; production comes last after analysis. |
| 20 | B | Feeling your English sounds "flat" shows awareness of native nuance—a good sign of readiness for C2 study. |
