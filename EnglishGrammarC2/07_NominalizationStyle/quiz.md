## Answer Key

| # | Answer | Explanation |
|---|--------|-------------|
| 1 | C | Nominalization = converting verbs/adjectives to nouns ("discover" → "discovery"). |
| 2 | A | "The discovery of the drug's efficacy" is the nominalized academic form. |
| 3 | D | "It is believed that..." removes personal subjects entirely. |
| 4 | B | "Investigated the drug's efficacy" packs information densely. |
| 5 | C | "Discover" is the formal Latinate equivalent of phrasal "find out." |
| 6 | B | "Conducted a study" replaces the phrasal verb "carried out." |
| 7 | B | Methods sections describing procedures use passive to focus on actions. |
| 8 | A | "Implementation of utilization of methodology for investigation" is excessively nominal. |
| 9 | C | "We" (author's we) is acceptable and common in academic writing. |
| 10 | D | Academic writing requires hedging: "suggests," "may contribute." |
| 11 | D | Contractions should be avoided in formal academic writing. |
| 12 | B | "In the given article, we consider the question of..." transfers Russian academic style. |
| 13 | C | Pre-modification: "recently carried out study" → "a recent study." |
| 14 | A | Methods use past tense ("were analyzed") and passive voice. |
| 15 | C | "Show" (Germanic) → "indicate" (Latinate) is the formal shift. |
| 16 | D | "Temperature" is the head noun; others are pre-modifiers. |
| 17 | A | Combining related ideas into one flowing sentence is better academic style. |
| 18 | D | Discussion sections mix tenses and use hedging for implications. |
| 19 | D | "Lots of" → "many" or "numerous" for formal register. |
| 20 | D | Balance is key—too much nominalization makes prose unclear. |
