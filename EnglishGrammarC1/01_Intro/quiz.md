## Answer Key

| # | Answer | Explanation |
|---|--------|-------------|
| 1 | B | C1 is about making appropriate choices based on context, not just knowing more rules. The same structure may be right or wrong depending on situation. |
| 2 | B | "I am agree" comes from Russian "Я согласен" where agreement is expressed with a predicate adjective requiring copula. In English, "agree" is a verb and doesn't need "be". |
| 3 | B | At B2, communication is successful despite errors. Without corrective feedback, errors persist. This is the intermediate plateau. |
| 4 | B | While grammatically correct, "Would you be so kind as to assist me in this matter?" is overly formal for most everyday situations. It sounds sarcastic or Victorian. |
| 5 | A | Russian has no article system at all. Russian speakers must learn to use articles from scratch, making them the most difficult feature. |
| 6 | C | "Depends from" is a calque from Russian "зависит от" (от = from). In English, "depend" takes the preposition "on". |
| 7 | B | Fossilization means errors become habits that are resistant to correction. They're repeated so often they feel natural to the learner. |
| 8 | B | Corpora show actual usage patterns, collocations, and frequency. They reveal what native speakers say, not just what textbooks prescribe. |
| 9 | B | "Noticing" (Schmidt) means consciously paying attention to language forms in input. It's necessary for acquisition of grammar. |
| 10 | B | Register is language variety appropriate to context. Formal register for academic writing, informal for friends, etc. |
| 11 | B | This inverted/fronted structure is "marked" — it draws attention to "tired" for emphasis. Unmarked would be "She was tired after the long journey." |
| 12 | C | We say "make an effort" not "do an effort". This is a fixed collocation. "Do" and "make" are not interchangeable. |
| 13 | B | In Russian present tense, the copula (быть) is omitted: "Она красивая" = "She (is) beautiful". This transfers to English as missing "is". |
| 14 | B | CEFR C1 describes "consistent grammatical control of complex language" where "errors are rare and difficult to spot." Not perfect, but highly controlled. |
| 15 | B | Articles are the number one problem area for Russian speakers because Russian has no article system. This affects nearly every sentence. |
| 16 | B | Deliberate practice focused on specific problems is more effective than general exposure at advanced levels. One problem at a time. |
| 17 | B | An error log helps identify YOUR specific patterns. Different learners have different error profiles; you need to know yours. |
| 18 | B | You can read/listen extensively but not notice specific forms. To acquire grammar, you need to pay attention to how things are said. |
| 19 | B | C1 speakers can flexibly switch between registers — using formal language when needed and informal when appropriate. |
| 20 | B | This course focuses on nuanced choices, context-dependent usage, and specifically addresses Russian L1 interference patterns. |
