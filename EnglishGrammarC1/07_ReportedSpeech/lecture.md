## 1. Backshift: When and Why

### 1.1 The Basic Rule

When the reporting verb is **past**, tenses "shift back":

```
Direct: "I am tired."
Reported: She said (that) she was tired.

Direct: "I have finished."
Reported: He said (that) he had finished.
```

### 1.2 The Backshift Table

| Direct Speech | Reported Speech |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Present Simple | → Past Simple |
| Present Continuous | → Past Continuous |
| Present Perfect | → Past Perfect |
| Past Simple | → Past Perfect |
| Past Continuous | → Past Perfect Continuous |
| will | → would |
| can | → could |
| may | → might |

### 1.3 When NOT to Backshift

**1. Timeless truths / general facts:**
```
She said (that) the Earth is round. (still true)
He explained that water boils at 100°C.
```

**2. Still true at reporting time:**
```
He said he lives in London. (he still does)
She told me she works at Google. (still working there)
```

**3. Just said / recent:**
```
He just said he's coming.
She says she doesn't understand.
```

**4. Hypothetical/unreal:**
```
"If I had money, I would help."
→ He said if he had money, he would help. (no change needed)
```

### 1.4 The Choice Principle

Backshift is not always obligatory. It's a **choice** based on:

```
BACKSHIFT: Distance from original — time has passed, situation may have changed
"She said she WAS tired." (reporting something from earlier)

NO BACKSHIFT: Relevance now — still true, recent
"She said she IS tired." (still relevant/true)
```

## 2. Reporting Verbs

### 2.1 Say vs Tell

**SAY:** + (that) clause / + direct speech
```
"She said (that) she was leaving."
"He said, 'I'll be late.'"
❌ "She said me that..."
```

**TELL:** + person + (that) clause
```
"She told me (that) she was leaving."
"He told them to wait."
❌ "She told that..."
```

### 2.2 Common Reporting Verbs

**Statements:**
```
say, tell, explain, mention, state, claim, announce, 
admit, confess, deny, add, reply, answer
```

**Questions:**
```
ask, inquire, wonder, want to know
```

**Commands/Requests:**
```
tell, ask, order, command, instruct, beg, urge, warn, advise
```

**Suggestions:**
```
suggest, recommend, propose, advise
```

### 2.3 Verb + That Clause

```
He claimed that he was innocent.
She admitted that she had made a mistake.
They announced that the project was cancelled.
The report states that sales have increased.
```

### 2.4 Verb + Object + Infinitive

For commands, requests, advice:

```
"Don't touch that!" → She told him not to touch it.
"Please help me." → She asked me to help her.
"You should see a doctor." → She advised me to see a doctor.
"Don't be late!" → She warned them not to be late.
```

### 2.5 Verb + -ing

Some verbs take gerund:

```
He admitted stealing the money. (= admitted that he had stolen)
She denied being there. (= denied that she was there)
They suggested going to a restaurant. (= suggested that we go)
He recommended reading this book.
```

## 3. Reporting Questions

### 3.1 Yes/No Questions

Use **if** or **whether**:

```
"Are you coming?" → She asked if/whether I was coming.
"Did you see him?" → He asked if/whether I had seen him.
"Will it rain?" → She wondered if/whether it would rain.
```

**Note:** Statement word order, no question mark:
```
❌ "She asked if was I coming?"
✅ "She asked if I was coming."
```

### 3.2 Wh- Questions

Keep the question word, use statement order:

```
"Where do you live?" → He asked where I lived.
"What time is it?" → She asked what time it was.
"Why did you leave?" → They wanted to know why I had left.
```

### 3.3 Common Errors

```
❌ She asked where do I live.
✅ She asked where I lived.

❌ He asked what was my name.
✅ He asked what my name was.

❌ She asked if can I help.
✅ She asked if I could help.
```

## 4. Reporting Commands and Requests

### 4.1 Structure

**Verb + Object + (not) to + infinitive**

```
"Wait here." → He told me to wait there.
"Don't move!" → She ordered them not to move.
"Please help." → He asked me to help.
"Could you close the door?" → She asked me to close the door.
```

### 4.2 Verb Choice

| Direct | Reporting Verb |
|--------|---------------|
| Neutral command | tell |
| Strong command | order, command |
| Request | ask |
| Strong request | beg, urge |
| Advice | advise, recommend |
| Warning | warn |

### 4.3 Examples

```
"Go away!" → He ordered me to go away.
"Please, please help me!" → She begged him to help her.
"I wouldn't do that if I were you." → He advised me not to do it.
"Be careful! It's dangerous." → She warned them to be careful.
```

## 5. Reporting Suggestions and Offers

### 5.1 Suggest

**Several patterns:**

```
"Let's go to the cinema."
→ She suggested going to the cinema.
→ She suggested (that) we (should) go to the cinema.
→ She suggested (that) we went to the cinema. (BrE informal)

"Why don't you try again?"
→ He suggested (that) I (should) try again.
→ He suggested trying again.
```

**Note:** "Suggest" never takes object + infinitive:
```
❌ She suggested me to go.
✅ She suggested (that) I go.
✅ She suggested going.
```

### 5.2 Offer and Promise

```
"I'll help you."
→ He offered to help me.
→ He promised to help me.

"Shall I carry that?"
→ She offered to carry it.
```

### 5.3 Agree and Refuse

```
"OK, I'll do it."
→ He agreed to do it.

"No, I won't help."
→ She refused to help.
```

## 6. Time and Place References

### 6.1 Changes

When reporting from a different time/place:

| Direct | Reported |
|--------|----------|
| now | then, at that time |
| today | that day |
| yesterday | the day before, the previous day |
| tomorrow | the next day, the following day |
| this week | that week |
| last week | the week before, the previous week |
| next week | the following week |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| these | those |

### 6.2 When to Change

Change only if the reference point has shifted:

```
Monday: "I'll call you tomorrow."
Tuesday (reporting): She said she would call me today.
Friday (reporting): She said she would call me on Tuesday.
```

## 7. Reporting in Academic Writing

### 7.1 Reporting Research

Academic writing uses reporting verbs extensively:

```
NEUTRAL:
Smith (2020) states/notes/observes that...
The study found/showed/demonstrated that...
The authors report/claim/suggest that...

AGREEMENT:
Jones (2019) confirms/supports/corroborates...
The data confirm/validate that...

DISAGREEMENT:
Brown (2021) challenges/disputes/questions...
However, Lee (2020) argues against this view...

TENTATIVE:
The researchers suggest/imply/indicate that...
It has been suggested that...
```

### 7.2 Tense in Academic Reporting

**Present for current relevance:**
```
Smith (2020) argues that climate change is accelerating.
(His argument is still relevant)
```

**Past for historical reference:**
```
Darwin (1859) proposed that species evolve.
(What he did at the time)
```

### 7.3 Integral vs Non-Integral Citations

**Integral (author in sentence):**
```
Smith (2020) found that...
According to Brown (2019),...
```

**Non-integral (author in parentheses):**
```
Recent studies have shown that... (Smith, 2020).
This phenomenon has been documented (Brown, 2019; Lee, 2020).
```

## 8. Common Errors

### 8.1 Word Order in Questions

```
❌ She asked where was he going.
✅ She asked where he was going.

❌ He wanted to know what did I think.
✅ He wanted to know what I thought.
```

### 8.2 Suggest Patterns

```
❌ She suggested me to try again.
✅ She suggested (that) I try again.
✅ She suggested trying again.
```

### 8.3 Say vs Tell

```
❌ She said me that she was tired.
✅ She told me (that) she was tired.
✅ She said (that) she was tired.
```

### 8.4 Unnecessary That

"That" is often optional and can be omitted in informal speech:
```
She said (that) she was coming.
He told me (that) he'd call.
```

### 8.5 Pronoun Confusion

```
Direct: "I will help you."
→ He told me he would help me. (NOT "he would help you")
```

## 💡 Key Takeaways

- **Backshift is a choice**: Consider whether information is still true/relevant
- **Vary reporting verbs**: Don't use just "said" — choose verbs for nuance
- **Question word order**: Statement order in reported questions (no inversion)
- **Suggest patterns**: Never "suggest someone to do" — use gerund or that-clause
- **Academic reporting**: Use appropriate verbs to show stance toward sources
