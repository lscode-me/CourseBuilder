## Answer Key

| # | Answer | Explanation |
|---|--------|-------------|
| 1 | A | Negative inversion: Negative adverb + auxiliary + subject: "Never have I seen." |
| 2 | D | "Always" is positive, not negative/restrictive. It doesn't trigger inversion. |
| 3 | A | "Only" + adverbial triggers inversion: "Only after X did I realize." |
| 4 | C | Conditional inversion: "Had + subject" = "If + subject + had": "Had I known." |
| 5 | B | It-cleft for focus on agent: "It was Mary who called yesterday." |
| 6 | B | What-cleft: "What [subject verb] is [focused element]." |
| 7 | D | "Not only + inversion in first clause": "Not only did she pass..." |
| 8 | A | Were-inversion for second conditional: "Were she here" = "If she were here." |
| 9 | A | Should-inversion for first conditional: "Should you need" = "If you should need." |
| 10 | D | Negative inversion is formal; appropriate for academic essays, not casual speech. |
| 11 | D | So + adjective + inversion: "So complex was the problem that..." |
| 12 | C | Emphatic DO is used to contradict doubt or emphasize truth. |
| 13 | D | Fronting "This" links the sentence to what came before (cohesion). |
| 14 | D | "All" clefts emphasize the completeness/simplicity of what's needed. |
| 15 | C | Overusing inverted structures sounds pompous or stilted, not sophisticated. |
| 16 | C | Adverbial fronting with intransitive verbs of position often triggers inversion. |
| 17 | B | "The reason" pseudo-cleft: "The reason [clause] was [focused element]." |
| 18 | C | Participial fronting creates a reduced adverbial clause, adding background info. |
| 19 | B | Russian speakers either underuse (flat) or overuse (pompous) when they learn them. |
| 20 | C | Formal report is the appropriate context; casual uses in A, C, D sound unnatural. |
