## 1. Defining vs Non-Defining Clauses

### 1.1 The Core Difference

**DEFINING (Restrictive):** Identifies which one we mean. Essential information.
```
"The man who called you is waiting outside."
(Which man? The one who called. Essential for identification.)
```

**NON-DEFINING (Non-restrictive):** Adds extra information. Could be removed.
```
"My brother, who lives in London, is visiting next week."
(I only have one brother. The clause adds extra info, not essential.)
```

### 1.2 Punctuation

**CRUCIAL:** Non-defining clauses need **commas**:

```
DEFINING (no commas):
"Students who work hard usually succeed."
(Not all students — only those who work hard)

NON-DEFINING (commas):
"My students, who work hard, usually succeed."
(All my students — and by the way, they work hard)
```

### 1.3 That vs Which

**In defining clauses:** "That" and "which" are interchangeable (for things)
```
"The book that/which I bought is interesting."
```

**In non-defining clauses:** Only "which" (never "that")
```
✅ "My car, which I bought last year, is very reliable."
❌ "My car, that I bought last year, is very reliable."
```

### 1.4 Meaning Difference

Compare:
```
"My sister who lives in Paris is a doctor."
(Implies: I have other sisters; this one lives in Paris)

"My sister, who lives in Paris, is a doctor."
(Implies: I have one sister; she happens to live in Paris)
```

## 2. Relative Pronouns

### 2.1 Overview

| Pronoun | For | Function | Example |
|---------|-----|----------|---------|
| who | people | subject | The man **who** called... |
| whom | people | object (formal) | The man **whom** I met... |
| which | things | subject/object | The book **which** is on the table... |
| that | people/things | subject/object | The person/thing **that** I saw... |
| whose | possession | possessive | The man **whose** car was stolen... |
| where | places | location | The house **where** I grew up... |
| when | time | time reference | The day **when** we met... |

### 2.2 Subject vs Object Pronouns

**Subject pronoun (cannot be omitted):**
```
"The man who called you..." (who = subject of "called")
"The book which is on the table..." (which = subject of "is")
```

**Object pronoun (can be omitted in defining clauses):**
```
"The man (who/whom/that) I met..." (I = subject, who = object)
"The book (which/that) I bought..." (I = subject, which = object)
```

### 2.3 Whom

"Whom" is formal/literary but still used after prepositions:

```
INFORMAL: "The person who I spoke to..."
FORMAL: "The person to whom I spoke..."

INFORMAL: "The man who I gave the book to..."
FORMAL: "The man to whom I gave the book..."
```

### 2.4 Whose

Shows possession, works for people AND things:

```
PEOPLE: "The woman whose car was stolen..."
THINGS: "The company whose profits increased..."
        "The house whose roof was damaged..."
```

### 2.5 Where, When, Why

```
WHERE: "The city where I was born..."
       = "The city in which I was born..."

WHEN: "The day when we met..."
      = "The day on which we met..."

WHY: "The reason (why) I called..."
     (often omitted)
```

## 3. Omitting the Relative Pronoun

### 3.1 When You Can Omit

Object pronouns in **defining clauses** can be omitted:

```
"The film (that/which) I saw was excellent."
"The person (who/that) I called didn't answer."
"The place (where) we visited was beautiful."
```

### 3.2 When You Cannot Omit

**Subject pronouns:**
```
❌ "The man called you is here."
✅ "The man who called you is here."
```

**Non-defining clauses:**
```
❌ "My brother, lives in London, is visiting."
✅ "My brother, who lives in London, is visiting."
```

## 4. Reduced Relative Clauses

### 4.1 Participle Clauses

Relative clauses can be reduced to **participle clauses**:

**Present participle (-ing) for active:**
```
"The man who is standing there is my boss."
→ "The man standing there is my boss."

"People who live in glass houses shouldn't throw stones."
→ "People living in glass houses shouldn't throw stones."
```

**Past participle (-ed/V3) for passive:**
```
"The book which was written by Tolstoy is very long."
→ "The book written by Tolstoy is very long."

"The car which was parked outside has been stolen."
→ "The car parked outside has been stolen."
```

### 4.2 Reduction Rules

**Can reduce when:**
- The relative pronoun is the subject
- Verb is in continuous or passive
- The meaning remains clear

```
CONTINUOUS: who is working → working
PASSIVE: which was built → built
PERFECT: who had left → having left (literary)
```

### 4.3 Examples

```
"Anyone who wants to come should register."
→ "Anyone wanting to come should register."

"The products which are made in Japan are reliable."
→ "The products made in Japan are reliable."

"The police interviewed everyone who had witnessed the accident."
→ "The police interviewed everyone having witnessed the accident." (formal)
```

## 5. Sentence Relatives

### 5.1 Which Referring to a Clause

"Which" can refer to a whole preceding clause:

```
"She passed the exam, which surprised everyone."
(which = the fact that she passed)

"He didn't call, which worried me."
(which = the fact that he didn't call)
```

### 5.2 Common Error

Don't use "what" for this:
```
❌ "She passed, what surprised everyone."
✅ "She passed, which surprised everyone."
```

## 6. Participle Clauses (Independent)

### 6.1 Present Participle Clauses

Express reason, time, or simultaneous action:

```
REASON: "Being tired, she went to bed early."
        = "Because she was tired..."

TIME: "Arriving at the station, I bought a ticket."
      = "When I arrived..."

SIMULTANEOUS: "She sat reading a book."
              = "She sat and read a book."
```

### 6.2 Past Participle Clauses

Often express reason or condition:

```
REASON: "Confused by the instructions, he asked for help."
        = "Because he was confused..."

CONDITION: "Heated to 100°C, water boils."
           = "When heated..."
```

### 6.3 Perfect Participle Clauses

For action completed before the main clause:

```
"Having finished his work, he went home."
= "After he had finished..."

"Having been warned, they were careful."
= "Because they had been warned..."
```

## 7. Common Errors

### 7.1 Punctuation

```
❌ "My mother who is 60 years old lives in Moscow."
✅ "My mother, who is 60 years old, lives in Moscow."
(Non-defining — you have only one mother)

❌ "The book, that I read, was interesting."
✅ "The book that I read was interesting."
(Defining — no commas, and can use "that")
```

### 7.2 That in Non-Defining

```
❌ "My car, that is very old, needs repair."
✅ "My car, which is very old, needs repair."
```

### 7.3 What vs Which

```
❌ "He resigned, what surprised everyone."
✅ "He resigned, which surprised everyone."

BUT "what" = "the thing that":
✅ "I don't understand what you mean."
   (what = the thing that you mean)
```

### 7.4 Whose vs Who's

```
❌ "The man who's car was stolen..."
✅ "The man whose car was stolen..."

(who's = who is; whose = possessive)
```

### 7.5 Participle Dangling

The subject of the participle must match the main clause subject:

```
❌ "Walking down the street, a car hit me."
   (Was the car walking? No!)
✅ "Walking down the street, I was hit by a car."
   (I was walking)

❌ "Having finished dinner, the TV was turned on."
✅ "Having finished dinner, we turned on the TV."
```

## 8. Style: When to Use What

### 8.1 Register Considerations

**Formal/Written:**
- Full relative clauses
- "Whom" after prepositions
- Non-defining clauses for additional detail

**Informal/Spoken:**
- Omit pronouns where possible
- "Who" instead of "whom"
- Simpler structures

### 8.2 Academic Writing

```
FORMAL: "The theory, which was proposed by Einstein, revolutionized physics."

MORE CONCISE: "Einstein's theory revolutionized physics."

WITH PARTICIPLE: "The theory proposed by Einstein revolutionized physics."
```

---

## 💡 Key Takeaways

- **Defining vs Non-defining**: Essential info (no commas) vs extra info (commas)
- **That vs Which**: "That" only in defining clauses; "which" in both
- **Omission**: Object pronouns can be omitted in defining clauses
- **Reduction**: Replace relative clause with participle for conciseness
- **Dangling participles**: Match subjects carefully
