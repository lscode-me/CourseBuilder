## Answer Key

| # | Answer | Explanation |
|---|--------|-------------|
| 1 | A | "If I have" = open possibility (1st conditional). "If I had" = hypothetical/unreal (2nd conditional). The past form signals unreality. |
| 2 | D | No "will" in if-clause for future conditions. Use Present Simple after "if" for future meaning. |
| 3 | C | Second conditional: If + Past Simple → would + infinitive. Hypothetical present situation. |
| 4 | D | Third conditional: If + Past Perfect → would have + past participle. Hypothetical past situation. |
| 5 | B | In formal English and fixed expressions, use "were" for all persons in unreal conditions. "If I were you" is always preferred. |
| 6 | C | Mixed conditional: Past condition (had studied) → Present result (would be). Past decision affects present state. |
| 7 | B | Mixed conditional: Present condition (weren't shy — she IS shy) → Past result (would have spoken). |
| 8 | B | Wish + Past Simple for present unreal situations. "I wish I spoke" = I don't speak, but I want to. |
| 9 | D | Wish + Past Perfect for past regrets. "I wish I had taken" = I didn't take it, I regret it. |
| 10 | D | "Wish + would" is for other people's actions, not your own. Say "I wish I could speak" not "I wish I would speak." |
| 11 | D | Subjunctive: base verb after "suggest." "That he attend" (not "attends"). |
| 12 | A | Subjunctive with "be": stays as "be" after "essential that." Doesn't become "is." |
| 13 | D | Unless = if not. "Unless you study" = "If you don't study." No double negative. |
| 14 | D | "If only I had listened" uses Past Perfect = regret about past. I didn't listen, I regret it. |
| 15 | A | Zero conditional (If + present → present) is for facts, general truths, scientific laws. |
| 16 | B | "Would" is never used in the if-clause of 2nd conditional. Should be "If I had more time, I would help." |
| 17 | A | "But for" + past context → would have + past participle. Implies without your help, failure would have occurred. |
| 18 | A | "Provided that" = on condition that, similar to "if" but more formal. |
| 19 | C | Third conditional expresses contrary-to-fact past. The speaker didn't know, so they didn't bake. |
| 20 | B | Second conditional: If + Past Simple → would + infinitive. "If I knew... I would tell." |
